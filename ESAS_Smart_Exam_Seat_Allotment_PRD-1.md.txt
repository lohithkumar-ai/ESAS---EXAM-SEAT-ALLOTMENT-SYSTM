# ESAS --- Smart Exam Seat Allotment System

## Single Product Requirements Document (PRD)

**Version:** 1.0\
**Project:** Exam Seat Allotment System (ESAS)\
**Domain:** Polytechnic / Diploma Examination Management\
**Target Institution:** AP Diploma / SBTET-style examination workflow\
**Primary Branches:** CME, ECE, EEE, ME\
**Room Capacity:** 42 students per room\
**Default Room Layout:** 3 columns × 14 rows\
**Primary Seating Pattern:** Zig-Zag / Serpentine\
**Primary User:** College Exam Cell Staff / Admin

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 1. Executive Summary

ESAS is a web-based examination seat-allotment platform for
diploma/polytechnic colleges. It automates the preparation of
examination seating arrangements from the Nominal Roll (NR) supplied by
the college examination staff.

The system is designed specifically for situations where:

-   Multiple diploma curricula exist, such as C23, C20, C16 and other
    applicable curricula.
-   Students belong to different years and semesters.
-   Regular and supplementary students appear in the same examination
    session.
-   Different branches have different subjects.
-   Some examinations may have the same subject across multiple
    branches, especially first-year/common-subject examinations.
-   The college has only a limited number of rooms, normally around 5--6
    rooms initially.
-   Each room has a maximum capacity of 42 students.
-   The objective is to mix CME, ECE, EEE and ME students as much as
    possible rather than placing one branch together.
-   If one branch cannot supply enough students for its planned
    positions, another available branch can substitute those positions.
-   Seating must be generated from the uploaded NR list rather than
    relying on hard-coded subject assumptions.
-   Regular and supplementary students must remain logically separated
    while still being handled within the same examination workflow.

The system contains two examination software modes:

1.  **March / April Examination Software**
    -   Includes 1st, 2nd and 3rd-year examination data as provided in
        the NR.
    -   Regular and supplementary students may be present.
    -   Applicable curricula and semesters are determined from the NR.
2.  **October / November Examination Software**
    -   Includes 2nd and 3rd-year examination data as provided in the
        NR.
    -   Regular and all years studeents supplementary students may be present.
    -   Applicable curricula and semesters are determined from the NR.

The user selects the required examination software from the main
website. Each mode opens its own examination workspace while sharing the
same core platform architecture.

# 2. Problem Statement

Manual examination seating is difficult when a college has:

-   Several curricula.
-   Multiple years and semesters.
-   Regular and supplementary students.
-   Four or more branches.
-   Different subjects for different branches.
-   Common subjects for some years.
-   Different examination sessions during the year.
-   Limited rooms.
-   Large or changing student counts.

Manual allocation can cause:

-   Students from the same branch sitting together.
-   Students appearing for the same subject being placed incorrectly.
-   Regular and supplementary students being mixed incorrectly.
-   Duplicate seat numbers.
-   Missing students.
-   Room over-capacity.
-   Incorrect attendance sheets.
-   Incorrect seating charts.
-   Difficult manual changes.
-   Errors in invigilator duty allocation.

ESAS solves these problems by using the NR as the authoritative source
and automatically calculating a valid seating arrangement.

# 6. Most Important Business Rule --- NR Is the Source of Truth

The system must allocate seats based on the uploaded Nominal Roll.

It must not make assumptions such as:

> "All CME students have Subject X."

Instead:

1.  Read every NR record.
2.  Identify PIN.
3.  Identify name.
4.  Identify branch.
5.  Identify year.
6.  Identify semester.
7.  Identify curriculum.
8.  Identify subject code.
9.  Identify subject name.
10. Identify Regular/Supplementary status.
11. Build the examination candidate pool.
12. Apply the seating algorithm.

This is critical because different curricula and years may have
different subjects.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 7. First-Year Common Subject Handling

First-year students require special handling.

It is possible that:

-   CME, ECE, EEE and ME have the same subject.
-   Only some branches have the same subject.
-   All four branches have different subjects.
-   Regular and supplementary students may have different subject
    combinations.

Therefore, the system must **detect subject collisions from the NR**.

## Example

If the NR shows:

  Branch   Subject
  -------- -------------
  CME      Mathematics
  ECE      Mathematics
  EEE      Mathematics
  ME       Mathematics

The system must understand that this is a common-subject group.

It must not artificially treat these students as four different subjects
merely because their branches differ.

If the NR shows:

  Branch   Subject
  -------- -------------
  CME      Mathematics
  ECE      Physics
  EEE      Mathematics
  ME       English

The system must preserve these differences.

## Allocation Principle

The algorithm must prioritize:

1.  Valid examination eligibility.
2.  No duplicate student.
3.  Correct subject/session.
4.  Branch mixing.
5.  Maximum practical distribution across branches.
6.  Room capacity.
7.  No invalid seating conflict.

# 8. Regular and Supplementary Handling

Regular and Supplementary students must be logically separated.

The system must maintain:

-   Regular candidate pool.
-   Supplementary candidate pool.

Reports must clearly identify the category.

However, the system should support mixed room allocation when the
examination rules allow it.

### Important rule

If a supplementary student belongs to a curriculum that is also
represented in the regular examination population, the system must still
classify that student as Supplementary.

Classification must be based on the NR's examination/category
information, not simply the curriculum.

The preferred allocation algorithm is a serpentine / zig-zag pattern.

Example conceptual order:

``` text
Column 1: Top → Bottom
Column 2: Bottom → Top
Column 3: Top → Bottom
Column 4: Bottom → Top
Column 5: Top → Bottom
Column 6: Bottom → Top
```


The exact seat numbering can be configured.

# 12. Branch Allocation Strategy

The system should attempt to distribute branches in this priority order:

### Priority 1

Use all four branches where possible.

### Priority 2

Avoid adjacent students from the same branch where possible.

### Priority 3

Avoid placing the same subject together where the examination rules
require separation.

### Priority 4

If a planned branch position has no candidate available, substitute
another available branch.

Example:

``` text
Required position:
CME

Available:
CME = 0
ECE = 5
EEE = 8
ME  = 6

Result:
Use ECE/EEE/ME substitution.
```

This is the required fallback behavior.

# 13. Subject-Aware Allocation

The algorithm must not only consider branches.

It must consider:

``` text
Curriculum
Year
Semester
Branch
Subject Code
Subject Name
Exam Type
Regular/Supplementary
```

The system should create a candidate identity such as:

``` text
Exam Session
+ Curriculum
+ Year
+ Semester
+ Subject
+ Category
+ Branch
```

The allocation engine then decides which candidates can safely share the
same room.

# 14. Room Allocation Logic

Input:

``` text
Total candidates
Room capacity = 42
```

Calculate:

``` text
required_rooms = ceil(total_candidates / 42)
```

Example:

``` text
84 students
84 / 42 = 2 rooms
```

Example:

``` text
95 students
95 / 42 = 2.26
Required rooms = 3
```

## Heavy Student Count

If the candidate population exceeds available room capacity:

**Automatically add another room.**

The system must never silently exceed 42 students in one room.

4.  Allow the administrator to add more rooms.
5.  Recalculate the allocation.

# 46. Allocation Engine Architecture

The allocation engine should be a separate service/module.

Suggested structure:

``` text
seating/
├── allocation/
│   ├── engine.py
│   ├── zigzag.py
│   ├── branch_balancer.py
│   ├── subject_validator.py
│   ├── room_allocator.py
│   └── validator.py
```

This prevents the business logic from being mixed directly into UI code.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 47. Allocation Engine Process

``` text
1. Load valid NR records
2. Filter by examination session
3. Normalize branches
4. Normalize curriculum
5. Normalize year
6. Normalize semester
7. Separate Regular/Supplementary
8. Detect subject groups
9. Calculate total capacity
10. Calculate required rooms
11. Create room/seat positions
12. Create branch-balanced candidate pools
13. Apply subject constraints
14. Apply zig-zag ordering
15. Substitute unavailable branch positions
16. Validate allocation
17. Generate final allocation
18. Save allocation
19. Generate reports
```

or:

``` text
CME → ME
```

The replacement should be selected from the largest suitable available
candidate pool while respecting subject/examination constraints.

The system should record substitutions for audit purposes.

Example:

``` text
Seat 13
Expected branch: CME
Actual branch: EEE
Reason: CME candidates exhausted
```

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 49. Common Subject Collision Strategy

The system must identify whether two or more branches have the same
subject.

Create a subject group:

``` text
Subject Code + Exam Session + Year + Semester + Curriculum
```

If multiple branches share the same subject:

``` text
CME → Mathematics
ECE → Mathematics
EEE → Mathematics
ME  → Mathematics
```

The engine marks this as:

``` text
COMMON SUBJECT GROUP
```

The algorithm then applies the configured institution rule.

If the institution requires students writing the same subject to be
separated as much as possible, the engine distributes them across
rooms/seats.

If common-subject students are permitted to share a room, the engine can
still branch-mix them while ensuring no duplicate student and correct
room capacity.

The rule must be configurable rather than hard-coded.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

filters.

examination portal, the integration must use an officially authorized

-   Authorized data export.
-   Official file exchange.

The application must not scrape, bypass authentication, or automate
access to a protected portal without authorization.

Until an official integration is available, the NR Excel upload remains
the authoritative input mechanism.

# 65. Final Product Definition

ESAS is not simply a random seat-number generator.

It is an **NR-driven, curriculum-aware, year-aware, semester-aware,
subject-aware, branch-balanced examination seating management system**.

Its central objective is:

> **Take the actual examination candidate list from the NR, understand
> Regular/Supplementary students across different curricula, years,
> semesters, branches and subjects, then automatically produce a valid
> 42-seat-per-room seating arrangement with maximum practical branch
> distribution and reliable examination reports.**

The two major examination workflows are:
